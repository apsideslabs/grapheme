The price fell because the model got smaller inside

Qwen4-Max and Hy4 flash both shipped today with seven-figure parameter counts and pennies-per-million prices. Neither cut was a discount.

Apsides Labs · 16 November 2026 · 8 min read · PRG-2026-015

Two Chinese labs put frontier models on sale this morning, and both lead with a number that is not the parameter count. Alibaba's Qwen4-Max activates 71 billion of its 2.4 trillion parameters. Tencent's Hy4 flash charges $0.070 per million input tokens and $0.003 for a cache hit.

Eight days ago we wrote about a price cut: Anthropic took Fable 5.2 from $10/$50 to $8/$40 and Google opened Gemini 4 Carbon at $2/$10. That was discounting — the same model, marked down. What happened today is different, and the difference matters more than the numbers.

What shipped

Qwen4-Max is the first model in Alibaba's Qwen4 generation. Alibaba describes it as a sparse mixture-of-experts model with 2.4 trillion total parameters and 71 billion active per token, native vision, and a context window of one million tokens. It says the model ranked sixth on Code Arena, and that on Work360 — Alibaba's own benchmark across 721 fields — it uses 35% fewer tokens than Qwen3.8-Max at high reasoning effort and cuts average cost per task by about 60%.

Tencent's Hy4 flash is the smaller, cheaper sibling of Hy4: 224 billion total parameters, 12 billion active per forward pass, one million token context, multimodal. Tencent's price list puts it at $0.070 per million input and $0.281 per million output, against $0.834 and $2.501 for the full Hy4 — about 92% and 89% lower. Cache hits cost $0.003.

Sparsity is the new discount

Both releases cut price by cutting the work per token, not by cutting the margin per unit of work. Alibaba's own framing is explicit: Qwen4-Max activates 25% fewer parameters than Qwen3.8-Max at the same 2.4 trillion scale, and still performs better on the benchmarks it cites. Tencent's Hy4 flash activates 12 billion of 224 billion.

That is the whole mechanism, and it is worth naming plainly. When a model's cost is driven by how many parameters it wakes up per token, architecture is a price lever. A better router is a price cut that does not have to be reversed when the promotion ends.

The price ladder, today

Set the numbers side by side and the scale becomes obvious. Claude Fable 5.2 is $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output. Gemini 4 Carbon is $2 and $10. Qwen4-Max's cached input is $0.075. Hy4 flash's input is $0.070, its cached input $0.003.

Two caveats before anyone re-plans a budget. These are list prices on launch, and Chinese labs have a history of promotional pricing that later moves. And input price is not cost per task: a cheaper model that takes twice as many turns to finish the job is not cheaper.

What it does not prove

Alibaba's Work360 is Alibaba's benchmark, run by Alibaba, and the "beats GPT-6.1-Sol and Claude Opus 5.5 on coding" line circulating today is the company's claim rather than an independent finding. Code Arena is one leaderboard. Tencent's most persuasive number is an internal blind test: 85 WorkBuddy users rating 259 tasks, where Hy4 flash scored close to Claude Haiku 5.5 on satisfaction at the lowest per-task cost. Useful, and still a vendor's study.

The honest position is that nobody outside these companies has rerun either model on a shared harness. That was true of every frontier launch this year, and it does not make the releases unimportant — it makes the benchmark tables promotional material.

The open-weights wrinkle

Qwen4-Max is proprietary. It is available through Alibaba's API and products, and the company says open-weight, smaller-scale Qwen4 models will be released to developers "soon". Tencent says it plans an open-weight Hy4 flash release.

That is the same sentence India heard three weeks ago about EKA-1, and the same one to check rather than believe. Yesterday's lesson was that open weights can still be closed in practice — a licence you can read, weights you can download, and a deployment guide that assumes hardware you do not own. A promise of open weights is a press release until the repository exists.

What to watch

Three things would settle it. An independent run of these models on a shared benchmark harness with the same tools and budget — the kind DeepMind's new MKA benchmark attempts, where the top models cost $31.97 and $6.29 per task respectively. A published cost-per-completed-task figure from someone who is not selling the model. And whether the open-weight tiers actually ship, or stay "soon" the way these things do.

The one-line version. For two years the price of a frontier token fell because labs competed on margin. This morning it fell because the models got more selective about which parameters they use. The first kind of cut can be withdrawn. The second cannot.

We wrote in The token got cheaper that neither of the November cuts told you what a finished task costs. That still holds. What changed is that one of the two levers now moves in the direction of the buyer without anyone having to take a loss to move it.
